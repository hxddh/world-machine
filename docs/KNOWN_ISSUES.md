# Known issues

Current as of `v0.27.0` in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md). Report anything else with **Help → Report a Problem…** in the app.

## Installation

- **Not notarized.** The first launch of a pre-alpha build needs the one-time "Open Anyway" step in [INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md). The pipeline is ready to sign and notarize; [RELEASE_SIGNING.md](RELEASE_SIGNING.md) lists the five secrets that switch it on.
- **No automatic download of updates.** Home shows a banner when a newer release exists (one request to GitHub per launch, see [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)); installing it is still download, drag onto Applications.
- **macOS 14 or newer only.** Older systems are not built or tested.

## Using Worlds

- **Worlds saved by an earlier Pack version do not open.** A World names the Pack version that saved it, and a build opens only the Pack versions it ships; `v0.19.0` opens no World saved by `v0.18.0`. Nothing is deleted; such a file still holds its whole history.

- **Removing a World that is open in a window is refused.** Close its window first. Renaming and exporting an open World from Home go through its window.
- **Removed Worlds are not deleted.** **Remove** moves the file into a `Removed` folder inside `~/Library/Application Support/World Machine/Worlds`. Emptying that folder is a Finder step; the app never deletes World files.
- **Background time is bounded.** A World advances one period for every six hours you were away, at most 28 periods per return (a week away); being away longer moves it no further. The return briefing says how many.
- **What people say is the Pack's own words, unless you talk to them with World voice on.** World voice narrates returns and, when it is on, answers for the people you talk to. The lines people say over their heads as they go about their day, and the three quick questions in their card, are always the Pack's own.
- **Without World voice, talking understands everyday phrasing, not everything.** People hear 28 kinds of thing in English and Chinese, and a 428-phrase corpus is heard without a miss. On new phrasing, measured on phrases written before any tuning, a quarter to nearly half were not understood and some were misheard; idioms ("bury the hatchet", "what makes you tick") are the usual misses. Anything not understood gets a hint. With World voice on, a model hears anything and picks from the same meanings. A fourth set of 61 new phrases, written for the `v0.14` review before any tuning, went 10% unheard, but was written by someone who knows the rules, so that is a floor, not a fair measure. For v0.27, new phrases were written before any tuning and half of them were kept out of it: of those 42 per language, 90.5% are understood in English, 88.1% in Chinese and 85.7% in Japanese.
- **World voice answers take a few seconds.** The window keeps moving and shows the person thinking. The model and its judge share one 11-second budget inside the window's 12-second deadline, and a request past its time is stopped; after that the World answers in its own words. This has been tried in tests with a fake slow model, not against a real model on a Mac.
- **People remember a month of what you said and did, and bring up one thing a day.** What they recall is the latest thing worth mentioning (something you said, an answer you gave them, something you made that they saw, a present), the first time they see you that day.
- **Only the core residents have voices of their own:** Tiny Society's eight and Pocket Universe's six. Newcomers speak the shared lines and take their five friendship moments from their two traits, so two newcomers with the same traits say the same things at those moments. Every resident's lines and scenes were generated and are held by tests, not written by a writer.
- **Letters come at most twice a week; a quiet day brings a first instead.** Firsts go round in turn (a corner of the place, someone's first word on a subject, their first word about someone else, an evening two people spent together) and the last kind never runs out, so after a year most firsts are evenings and changes between people. Keepsakes are at most three a week.
- **A return's keepsake comes from whoever likes you most,** when the week has room for one; otherwise the return ends on a letter.
- **Three years are measured, not played.** A scripted warm player's 1,080 days are the evidence for the ladder of works, the year turns, letters and quiet days in both Packs; nobody has lived a year in the harbour on a Mac. The 1,080-day tests take a minute or more and run on request (`cargo test -p tiny-society --lib long_run -- --ignored`); Pocket Universe's run by default. In Pocket Universe some turns fall to whoever is nearest: Nia has no apprentice, so Ines takes the greenhouse; Ray's kid stays off stage. What people say while resting on and gathering at things they made ("Whoever made this bench…") is the most repeated line in the third year.
- **A guest comes from the first other World in your library** that has people; there is no choosing yet, and a guest from a World whose cover keeps no names is "a neighbour".
- **World codes carry a season, not a whole history.** A `wm2:` code holds the World as it stood at the season's start and everything since (about 48,000 characters at three years), so a visit shows the World as it is, but its History, book and chapter book tell only the latest season. A `wm2:` code needs a Pack that speaks protocol v4 or later; an older installed Pack says it needs a newer one. A postcard of a past moment has that moment's words and day but today's scene. Neither has been tried on a Mac. `.worldcode` files are registered with Finder and open as a visit, but that has not been tried on a Mac either.
- **The strip is untried on a Mac.** Its pacing is tested (never 15 frames a second, nothing drawn while nothing moves); its CPU and energy use, the always-in-front panel and its borderless look are not. It shows the World as its open window last saw it, and one strip follows one window.
- **Festivals happen while you are away.** Like a storm, a festival's day does not wait for you; the return tells how it went.
- **A long absence brings its own story.** While you are away wants wait for you and people live their own lives, but what cannot wait (a storm, market day, a chapter's climax) happens without you and can close a chapter. Its pacing has only been tried in tests, not over two weeks on a real Mac.
- **Pocket Universe gives one reason for every answer it cannot take:** "Not possible right now". Tiny Society names what is short ("Noah hasn't 60 to spare").
- **History tells a World's recent past.** History is told from the latest 1,200 events, about two months of a busy World, and shows its latest 40 moments. What people did each day is told as it happens, in what they say; History keeps today's and what changed between people. A detail panel lists someone's twelve latest changes, and the chain of causes and detail panel of an event are there for the latest 400 events. Older events are still in the file, and the chapter book tells them.
- **Timings are not Mac numbers.** On the `v0.20.0` release build, measured on a busy Linux machine: opening a three-year Tiny Society World from its file takes 170–205 ms and it then holds 125–129 MB; a snapshot takes 18–28 ms and letting a day pass 23–35 ms. On a quieter run of the same machine the benchmarks gave 120–150 ms, under 100 MB and 13.6 ms. Each thing you do takes about 26–41 ms in the app, or 61–106 ms through the Pack program including the save, and Home lists ten such Worlds in about 45 ms. A World last saved before `v0.20.0` is read in full by Home until it is saved once. None of this has been timed on a real Mac.
- **This Mac's own model is untried.** The `fm` voice follows the form Apple documents for macOS 27 and is tested against recorded replies; it has not been run on a Mac, and Settings shows it only where `fm` answers a first question.
- **The drawings are generated, not an illustrator's.** People are drawn from outlines the code makes (48 in Tiny Society, 36 penguins), with faces by mood, and a question's portrait is the same drawing; buildings, fixtures and the things you make are flat shapes drawn in code, with contact shading, a foreground of grass, flowers and stones scattered by the program, and scaffolding on what is still being built. They have been checked as drawings and contact sheets, and on Linux, not on a Mac. A commissioned artist's set should replace them before `1.0`.
- **Zooming uses the scroll wheel only.** There is no pinch or on-screen control yet; Escape pulls back.
- **Your hands allow two deeds a period,** and in Pocket Universe they cost nothing, since its places keep no money. Something put anywhere on the ground still counts as being at the nearest place. Only the latest thing made or moved can be taken back, and only the same period.
- **Photo needs Screen Recording.** It saves the window with macOS's own `screencapture`, which the system may ask permission for the first time; it has not been tried on a Mac.
- **The calendar repeats by design.** Market day, supply drops and birthdays are left out of the tests that keep other questions to two in 30 periods and count how many follow from an answer.
- **Only the latest chapter's ending shows as a card,** and only if it closed within the last period; earlier ones are in the drawer's chapter book.
- **A living World redraws at the display's rate while its window is in front** (60 or 120 times a second; a few times a second with Reduce Motion). Behind other windows it stops. Its effect on battery life has not been measured on a real Mac.
- **The sky follows your clock, not the World's.** Dawn, dusk and night are drawn from the Mac's local time, like a window onto the same sky; a World records nothing about them.
- **No notification while a World is closed.** A World keeps going without you and its window says when it next moves, but nothing tells you from outside the app. macOS delivers notifications reliably only to a signed app, so this waits on notarization.

- **Chinese is generated.** The catalogs were translated by a model and checked by tests, not by a translator. Over three years no line a player sees in either Pack is left partly English; people keep their names in Latin letters and places and things have Chinese names. Some translations are weak (a colony is sometimes 殖民地 and sometimes 聚居地), and a line a World voice model says is shown as the model said it. Settings → Display switches back to English.
- **Japanese is generated too.** Every catalog has a Japanese version, written by a model in natural polite-casual Japanese and read back once, not by a translator. Residents' names are in katakana. Talking in Japanese is heard through about 110 everyday phrases; on 48 new ones, 5 were not understood and 1 was misheard before tuning. Nobody has played in Japanese on a Mac.
- **Text size, contrast and VoiceOver are unverified on a Mac.** Text from 100% to 200% is checked by tests on speech paging only; how every window lays out at 200%, what Increase Contrast looks like, and what VoiceOver reads have not been seen on a Mac.

- **The new look and sound are unseen and unheard on a Mac.** The painted scene is measured on Linux: the window's longest frame in a three-year benchmark is 10.5–12.4 ms, and a full background repaint takes 125–250 ms off the window's thread, faded in when ready. The zoomed-out overview of a three-year World took 27–47 ms a frame in a debug preview and has not been measured in release. Sound goes through CoreAudio via `cpal`, which has been compiled but never run on a Mac; the app logs the buffer the device grants and its delay on first sound. Bluetooth output adds 100–200 ms that no setting can remove. Nobody has listened to the new sound yet; `cargo test -p world-sound --release --test bars write_sounds -- --ignored` with `WORLD_MACHINE_SOUNDS=dir` writes it to files.
- **The core residents never die.** Tiny Society's eight and Pocket Universe's six are the ones who ask questions, host and speak at festivals, so they grow old and retire but do not die by chance. Deaths fall on newcomers who grew old. In a three-year Tiny Society World that means 2 deaths.
- **A legend gives a cause for about half its lines.** A cause is shown only when it is a real, earlier Event: an answer you gave, a festival or a storm. A friendship made or lost says why in its own words. Everyday beats with no such cause are shown without one.
- **Moment strips are saved with `screencapture`,** like Photo and postcards, so saving one needs Screen Recording and has not been tried on a Mac. Nobody has seen the ages, the legend page, the strips or the almanac on a Mac; they were checked on Linux.
- **A town at noon looks thin in any one view.** Everyone is outside by day, but spread along a panorama four and a half screens wide, so one screen holds three or four people.
- **The World voice still lets some bad answers through, and wrongly declines some good ones in Chinese and Japanese.** Its answers pass two layers: structural checks on every answer, and a judge model (the same key's cloud model, Claude Haiku 4.5 by default) that answers a checklist about whatever the checks are not certain of, from which the app decides. On a fifth red-team set of 2,806 lines, written blind in English, Chinese and Japanese and never seen while building either layer (`systems/conversation/tests/redteam5/README.md`):
  - with the judge, 1,132 of 1,244 out-of-world answers (91.0%) are declined and 25 of 1,562 good answers (1.6%) wrongly declined; per language 93.2% / 0.2% (en), 89.0% / 2.1% (zh) and 90.9% / 2.5% (ja);
  - the bar was at least 95% declined and at most 1% wrongly declined in each language. Only English's wrongly-declined figure meets it;
  - without a judge the strict rules alone decline 59.4% and wrongly decline 2.2% (en 63.7%, zh 56.4%, ja 58.5%), against a bar of 75% in each language.

  **What set 5 does not measure.** Its English part has no harmful lines, and neither its English nor its Chinese part has prompt-injection lines, so those kinds are measured there in the other languages only (sets 3 and 4 cover them in all three). The judge pair's other half, this Mac's own model as a veto, has not been measured at all, because there is no Mac to run it.

  **How it was measured.** The verdicts were recorded by giving each exact judge prompt to Claude Haiku 4.5 through agent sessions that could only read their batch and write their answers, not through the app's own request with a real key, so they are a close stand-in for the shipped judge rather than a measurement of it.

  **The v0.26 numbers for set 4 may not be trustworthy.** v0.26 published 91.6% declined and 0.6% wrongly declined on set 4 with Haiku as the judge. Some of those batches may have been answered by the judging agent running scripts over the prompts instead of reading them, so those numbers may not show what Haiku itself would say. For set 5, an earlier round judged that way was thrown away and every batch judged again by read-only agents. Set 4's recorded verdicts are still used as a floor that later releases must not fall below, but not as a measure of the judge.

  A declined answer is replaced by the World's own words, and what decided is recorded in the World, so replay never asks a model. World voice is off by default.
- **The `fm` helper is source only.** It has never been compiled (there is no Swift on the machines that build it outside a Mac), so neither its voice nor its judge mode has run. The release workflow tries to build it on the Mac runner and carries on without it.
- **Day 1 is still quiet.** A new harbour has washing on a line, a handcart on the quay and a rowboat or two moored off it from the first day (drawn where nothing the World built stands, and recorded nowhere), but it is mostly its back row, a meadow and the quay until the town builds.
- **The new look, the Pocket Universe places and the drawer are unseen on a Mac.** They were checked in the Linux preview and in golden images.
- **A World's file is written just after its turn, not during it.** A turn hands what it recorded to a writer that makes and writes the file (synced, the save before kept as `.bak`, renamed into place) a few milliseconds later, in order; closing the World or quitting the app waits for it. A crash in those milliseconds loses that turn, never the file. A file someone else changed is not written over, and the next turn says so. A turn with its save is now 31 ms (median; 19–55 ms) on a busy 4-core Linux machine at load 4, against 61 ms before; not measured on a Mac.
- **One app writes a World at a time.** While a World is open, a hidden lock file beside it (`.<name>.world.lock`) keeps any other window or World Machine (the demo and the full app share a Worlds folder) from opening it to play or renaming or removing it from Home; Home renames and exports an open World through its window. The operating system lets go of the lock if the app crashes. A sync tool or a copy put back by hand does not take the lock: the open World then refuses its next turn as "changed on disk", as before.
- **Three v0.27 speed and size bars are measured, not met.** On a three-year harbour, on a busy 4-core Linux machine (load 9–15), timed in turn against v0.26 on the same runs:
  - the first look after a day (the snapshot alone, now timed apart from the day) is 25–36 ms (v0.26: 27–38 ms; bar 20 ms);
  - the app holds 104–107 MB after 20 days of play (v0.26: 105–109 MB; bar 100 MB; 92–96 MB just opened);
  - a turn with its save is 51–70 ms (v0.26: 41–61 ms; bar 30 ms).

  The nightly reports all three without failing. A profile puts most of the first look in building the inspector, the moments, the commands and the town afresh; the live heap stays level over the 20 days, and the growth is the allocator's high-water mark after the first save, with the World's history held both as events and as the file's text.
- **A builder's three-year World code is 49,179 characters,** under the 50,000 bar by less than 2%. A denser code format would give it room.
- **At night the drawer, cards and pages are paper under a lamp** (in the light appearance), while what hangs over the scene (the gauges, buttons, names and speech) is dark as the night sky. Seen on Linux, not on a Mac.
- **Moment strips draw only the design of the place a moment is at** (a shop's sign, a home's quilt, a flag or a sail), not designs elsewhere in town.
- **Designing, naming, plots, the touchable strip and a friend's resident are untried on a Mac.** They were checked in the Linux preview, keys and clicks included.
- **Homes and works cannot be inspected.** They are drawn and named, but clicking one opens nothing, because they are worked out for the picture rather than recorded in the World.
- **A visitor on a World code does not see when works were built,** since a code carries only its latest season.
- **A favour is one at a time and asked only while you are there.** People ask you to look in on someone, invite someone out, cheer someone up or pass on an apology, and you do it by talking to that person or with the one-click reply in their card. A favour not done in three days lapses quietly, and asks slow down after lapses. Done in your own words, on phrasing written before any tuning, 90% of English favours are understood, but only 67% of Chinese and 75% of Japanese ones (12 phrases each; bar 90%).
- **The demo ends on day 21 of Tiny Society,** with a farewell at dusk. It is the full app otherwise, for Tiny Society only; the World it keeps opens in the full app and carries on. It has been built and run in the Linux preview, not on a Mac; the farewell scene itself has not been seen in the preview yet.

## Verification gaps

- The pre-alpha has been exercised through automated tests and CI-built screenshots, not yet on a wide range of real Macs. Layout on small screens and with large accessibility text sizes is unverified.
- **Nothing that needs a keypress or a click is verified anywhere.** The CI screenshot runner can launch the app and open a World, but GitHub's macOS runners grant no Accessibility permission, so no keystroke it sends ever arrives — proven by pressing ⌘M, which the app binds, and watching the window not minimize. Full screen, every keyboard shortcut, and every button click are therefore unchecked until somebody runs the app on a real Mac.
- **Linux preview is not a Mac.** Since `v0.6.0`, `scripts/linux-preview.sh` runs the real app under Xvfb and every screenshot in [PRODUCT_REVIEW.md](PRODUCT_REVIEW.md) comes from it, clicks and keys included. Fonts, weights and window chrome differ from macOS, and the Pack install review has not been seen at all, because the preview cannot drive a file picker.
- The CI screenshot runner produced images with layout but no text through `v0.5.0`. That was not a limitation of the runner: the app itself rendered no text anywhere, on CI and on real Macs alike, and `v0.5.1` fixes it. Screenshots in the repository are still taken by hand on a real Mac.
