# Known issues

Current as of `v0.29.0` in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md). Report anything else with **Help → Report a Problem…** in the app.

## Installation

- **Not notarized.** The first launch of a pre-alpha build needs the one-time "Open Anyway" step in [INSTALL.md](INSTALL.md). The pipeline is ready to sign and notarize; [RELEASE_SIGNING.md](RELEASE_SIGNING.md) lists the five secrets that switch it on. The Mac build stays ad-hoc signed for now by the owner's choice: there is no Apple Developer enrolment yet.
- **The Windows installer is unsigned and untried.** An optional release job builds an installer and a zip; they are not signed, so SmartScreen warns on first run, and nobody has installed or played them on a real Windows PC. CI runs clippy and the tests on Windows.
- **No automatic download of updates.** Home shows a banner when a newer release exists (one request to GitHub per launch, see [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md)); installing it is still download, drag onto Applications.
- **macOS 14 or newer only.** Older systems are not built or tested.

## Using Worlds

- **Worlds saved by an earlier Pack version do not open.** A World names the Pack version that saved it, and a build opens only the Pack versions it ships; `v0.19.0` opens no World saved by `v0.18.0`. Nothing is deleted; such a file still holds its whole history.

- **Removing a World that is open in a window is refused.** Close its window first. Renaming and exporting an open World from Home go through its window.
- **Removed Worlds are not deleted.** **Remove** moves the file into a `Removed` folder inside `~/Library/Application Support/World Machine/Worlds`. Emptying that folder is a Finder step; the app never deletes World files.
- **Background time is bounded.** A World advances one period for every six hours you were away, at most 28 periods per return (a week away); being away longer moves it no further. The return briefing says how many.
- **What people say is the Pack's own words, unless you talk to them with World voice on.** World voice narrates returns and, when it is on, answers for the people you talk to. The lines people say over their heads as they go about their day, and the three quick questions in their card, are always the Pack's own.
- **Without World voice, talking understands everyday phrasing, not everything.** People hear 28 kinds of thing in English, Chinese and Japanese, and a 428-phrase corpus is heard without a miss. Since v0.29 Japanese and Chinese spellings are folded before matching (katakana to hiragana, particles, spoken Chinese forms), and a small scored matcher catches words the table misses. On fresh29, a new set measured once at the end, 94.6% are understood in English, 87.5% in Chinese and 83.9% in Japanese, against a bar of 85% in each; fresh29 was written by the agent that tuned, before it read the code, so these numbers may be optimistic. Hearing is still mostly a phrase table; anything not understood gets a hint, and 2–3 suggested openers from the World's own facts sit beside the text field until you have said something that day. With World voice on, a model hears anything and picks from the same meanings, but what talk *does* (warmth, favours) is always decided by the World's own ears on your words.
- **World voice answers take a few seconds.** The window keeps moving and shows the person thinking. The model and its judge share one 11-second budget inside the window's 12-second deadline, and a request past its time is stopped; after that the World answers in its own words. This has been tried in tests with a fake slow model, not against a real model on a Mac.
- **People remember a month of what you said and did, and bring up one thing a day.** What they recall is the latest thing worth mentioning (something you said, an answer you gave them, something you made that they saw, a present), the first time they see you that day.
- **Only the core residents have voices of their own:** Tiny Society's eight and Pocket Universe's six. Newcomers speak the shared lines and take their five friendship moments from their two traits, so two newcomers with the same traits say the same things at those moments. Every resident's lines and scenes were generated and are held by tests, not written by a writer.
- **Letters come at most twice a week; a quiet day brings a first instead.** Firsts go round in turn (a corner of the place, someone's first word on a subject, their first word about someone else, an evening two people spent together) and the last kind never runs out, so after a year most firsts are evenings and changes between people. Keepsakes are at most three a week.
- **A return's keepsake comes from whoever likes you most,** when the week has room for one; otherwise the return ends on a letter.
- **Three years are measured, not played.** A scripted warm player's 1,080 days are the evidence for the ladder of works, the year turns, letters and quiet days in both Packs; nobody has lived a year in the harbour on a Mac. The 1,080-day tests take a minute or more each in release and run on request (`cargo test --release -p tiny-society -p pocket-universe --lib -- --ignored`, which includes the words and favour harnesses) and in the nightly; of Pocket Universe's, only "three years in every place keep moving" runs with every test run. In Pocket Universe some turns fall to whoever is nearest: Nia has no apprentice, so Ines takes the greenhouse; Ray's kid stays off stage. Lines about the bench and the swing are spoken only now and then, and none is in the third year's top 20 lines for any of five kinds of player; the most-said line is said 4–5 times in the third year.
- **A guest comes from the first other World in your library** that has people; there is no choosing yet, and a guest from a World whose cover keeps no names is "a neighbour".
- **World codes carry a season, not a whole history.** A `wm2:` code holds the World as it stood at the season's start and everything since (about 48,000 characters at three years), so a visit shows the World as it is, but its History, book and chapter book tell only the latest season. A `wm2:` code needs a Pack that speaks protocol v4 or later; an older installed Pack says it needs a newer one. A postcard of a past moment has that moment's words and day but today's scene. Neither has been tried on a Mac. `.worldcode` files are registered with Finder and open as a visit, but that has not been tried on a Mac either.
- **The strip is untried on a Mac.** Its pacing is tested (never 15 frames a second, nothing drawn while nothing moves); its CPU and energy use, the always-in-front panel and its borderless look are not. It shows the World as its open window last saw it, and one strip follows one window.
- **Festivals happen while you are away.** Like a storm, a festival's day does not wait for you; the return tells how it went.
- **A long absence brings its own story.** While you are away wants wait for you and people live their own lives, but what cannot wait (a storm, market day, a chapter's climax) happens without you and can close a chapter. Its pacing has only been tried in tests, not over two weeks on a real Mac.
- **Pocket Universe gives one reason for every answer it cannot take:** "Not possible right now". Tiny Society names what is short ("Noah hasn't 60 to spare").
- **History tells a World's recent past.** History is told from the latest 1,200 events, about two months of a busy World, and shows its latest 40 moments. What people did each day is told as it happens, in what they say; History keeps today's and what changed between people. A detail panel lists someone's twelve latest changes, and the chain of causes and detail panel of an event are there for the latest 400 events. Older events are still in the file, and the chapter book tells them.
- **Timings are not Mac numbers.** On v0.29 in release, on a three-year Tiny Society World on a shared 4-core Linux machine at load 1–2: letting a day pass takes about 4 ms and the first look at the World after it 19–37 ms (the bar is 20 ms); looking again at a World that has not changed takes about 3 ms; the app holds about 93 MB once the World is open and 108.6 MB after 20 days of play (the bar is 100 MB). A turn with its save is below. A World last saved before `v0.20.0` is read in full by Home until it is saved once. None of this has been timed on a real Mac.
- **This Mac's own model is untried.** The `fm` voice follows the form Apple documents for macOS 27 and is tested against recorded replies; it has not been run on a Mac, and Settings shows it only where `fm` answers a first question.
- **The drawings are generated, not an illustrator's.** People are drawn from outlines the code makes (48 in Tiny Society, 36 penguins), with faces by mood, and a question's portrait is the same drawing; buildings, fixtures and the things you make are flat shapes drawn in code, with contact shading, a foreground of grass, flowers and stones scattered by the program, and scaffolding on what is still being built. They have been checked as drawings and contact sheets, and on Linux, not on a Mac. A commissioned artist's set should replace them before `1.0`.
- **Zooming uses the scroll wheel only.** There is no pinch or on-screen control yet; Escape pulls back.
- **Your hands allow two deeds a period,** and in Pocket Universe they cost nothing, since its places keep no money. Something put anywhere on the ground still counts as being at the nearest place. Only the latest thing made or moved can be taken back, and only the same period.
- **Photo needs Screen Recording.** It saves the window with macOS's own `screencapture`, which the system may ask permission for the first time; it has not been tried on a Mac. Postcards capture the screen too, not an offscreen picture: through PowerShell on Windows (untried on a real PC), and ImageMagick's `import` on X11 or `grim` on Wayland on Linux.
- **The calendar repeats by design.** Market day, supply drops and birthdays are left out of the tests that keep other questions to two in 30 periods and count how many follow from an answer.
- **Only the latest chapter's ending shows as a card,** and only if it closed within the last period; earlier ones are in the drawer's chapter book.
- **A living World redraws at the display's rate while its window is in front** (60 or 120 times a second; a few times a second with Reduce Motion). Behind other windows it stops: on Linux, covered by a window that takes the focus, it draws nothing and uses about 1% of a core, and minimised about 0.2% (`scripts/measure-background.sh`). A covered window that keeps the input focus still draws. Under Xvfb's software renderer a World in front uses about 2.9 cores at 5 frames a second. Its effect on battery life has not been measured on a real Mac.
- **The sky follows your clock, not the World's.** Dawn, dusk and night are drawn from the Mac's local time, like a window onto the same sky; a World records nothing about them.
- **No notification while a World is closed.** A World keeps going without you and its window says when it next moves, but nothing tells you from outside the app. macOS delivers notifications reliably only to a signed app, so this waits on notarization.

- **Chinese is generated.** The catalogs were translated by a model and checked by tests, not by a translator. Over three years in every place, no letter shows an English sentence and no shown line is left partly English, in Chinese and Japanese alike, held by a test that checks every slot a translation uses. People's names are written in Chinese characters and places and things have Chinese names. Some translations are weak (a colony is sometimes 殖民地 and sometimes 聚居地), and a line a World voice model says is shown as the model said it, though a model answer in Traditional characters is declined. Lines break by CJK rules (no line starts with 。、」 or a small kana, no break inside a katakana name or a word such as 木材, Japanese between phrases). Settings → Display switches back to English.
- **Japanese is generated too.** Every catalog has a Japanese version, written by a model in natural polite-casual Japanese and read back once, not by a translator. Residents' names are in katakana. How well talking in Japanese is heard is measured with the other languages, above. Nobody has played in Japanese on a Mac.
- **Text size, contrast and VoiceOver are unverified on a Mac.** Text from 100% to 200% is checked by tests on speech paging only; how every window lays out at 200%, what Increase Contrast looks like, and what VoiceOver reads have not been seen on a Mac.

- **The new look and sound are unseen and unheard on a Mac.** The painted scene is measured on Linux. A release build of the real window (under Xvfb with software Vulkan) shows no unpainted picture after its first frame, which is itself whole: what is not yet painted sharp stands in as a rough painting of the World's own tiles, and since v0.29 the subject of a moment is painted first (`scripts/release-shots.sh`, run by the release workflow before anything is published). The window's longest frame is under its 8 ms bar in 6 of 10 local runs on a 4-core machine at v0.29 (6.3–13.2 ms, and one of 102 ms, about that run's 99 ms opening frame; the cause is not confirmed). Sound goes through CoreAudio via `cpal`, which has been compiled but never run on a Mac; the app logs the buffer the device grants and its delay on first sound. Bluetooth output adds 100–200 ms that no setting can remove. Nobody has listened to the new sound yet; `cargo test -p world-sound --release --test bars write_sounds -- --ignored` with `WORLD_MACHINE_SOUNDS=dir` writes it to files.
- **The core residents never die.** Tiny Society's eight and Pocket Universe's six are the ones who ask questions, host and speak at festivals, so they grow old and retire but do not die by chance. Deaths fall on newcomers who grew old. In a three-year Tiny Society World that means 2 deaths.
- **A legend gives a cause for about half its lines.** A cause is shown only when it is a real, earlier Event: an answer you gave, a festival or a storm. A friendship made or lost says why in its own words. Everyday beats with no such cause are shown without one.
- **Moment strips are saved with `screencapture`,** like Photo and postcards, so saving one needs Screen Recording and has not been tried on a Mac. Nobody has seen the ages, the legend page, the strips or the almanac on a Mac; they were checked on Linux.
- **A town at noon looks thin in any one view.** Everyone is outside by day, but spread along a panorama four and a half screens wide, so one screen holds three or four people.
- **The World voice still lets some bad answers through, and wrongly declines some good ones.** Its answers pass two layers: structural checks on every answer (now with a complete name list per World in three languages, era word lists per place, and certain declines for crisis topics, which are never sent to a model), and a judge model (the same key's cloud model, Claude Haiku 4.5 by default) that answers a twelve-question checklist about whatever the checks are not certain of, from which the app decides. On a sixth red-team set of 4,801 lines (1,799 out-of-world and 3,002 good), written blind in English, Chinese and Japanese under v0.28's policy (real places and events of the era are in the world; brands, celebrities, media titles and real currency are not, unless the World names them) and measured once at the end:
  - with the judge, 94.0% of out-of-world answers are declined and 2.4% of good answers wrongly declined; per language 94.7% / 4.0% (en), 94.3% / 2.3% (zh) and 93.0% / 0.9% (ja);
  - the bar was at least 95% declined and at most 1% wrongly declined in each language. Only Japanese's wrongly-declined figure meets it;
  - most misses are invented names and places (79 of 108) and things out of their time (22); most wrong declines are the judge reading nicknames ("the Skipper"), real places of the era or figures of speech as out of the world (58 of 72), and 11 are English answers quoting a resident's name in katakana;
  - without a judge, the certain and firm checks alone decline 33.1% and wrongly decline 0.5%.

  These numbers are from v0.28. v0.29 teaches the judge the policy with examples per place and lets a name the World gave pass the language check in any script, but its blind set 7 needs a live key and has not been measured, so the voice's figures are still v0.28's.

  **How it was measured.** The verdicts were recorded by giving each exact judge prompt to Claude Haiku 4.5 through read-only agent sessions, 25 lines a batch, not through the app's own request with a real key, so they are a close stand-in for the shipped judge rather than a measurement of it. The judge pair's other half, this Mac's own model as a veto, has not been measured at all. Sets 3, 4 and 5 are kept as floors on their recorded verdicts.

  **Talk never pays, almost.** What a conversation does is decided by the World's own ears on the player's words, never by a model's reading or reply; no trick in three languages (narrated outcomes, claims of a favour done, words said back, authority) can make a model or a reply complete a favour, move a thing or money, or change anything beyond how people feel. Since v0.29 looking in on someone is done only by words that ask after them. On v0.29's blind set, 1 of 3,348 tricks in Tiny Society still did something by the player's own words ("free drink" heard as an invitation out), and 0 of 1,116 in Pocket Universe; the bar was none.

  A declined answer is replaced by the World's own words, and what decided is recorded in the World, so replay never asks a model. World voice is off by default.
- **The `fm` helper is source only.** It has never been compiled (there is no Swift on the machines that build it outside a Mac), so neither its voice nor its judge mode has run. The release workflow tries to build it on the Mac runner and carries on without it.
- **Day 1 is still quiet.** A new harbour has washing on a line, a handcart on the quay and a rowboat or two moored off it from the first day (drawn where nothing the World built stands, and recorded nowhere), but it is mostly its back row, a meadow and the quay until the town builds.
- **The new look, the Pocket Universe places and the drawer are unseen on a Mac.** They were checked in the Linux preview and in golden images.
- **A World's file is written just after its turn, not during it.** A turn hands what it recorded to a writer that makes and writes the file (synced, the save before kept as `.bak`, renamed into place) a few milliseconds later, in order; closing the World or quitting the app waits for it. A crash in those milliseconds loses that turn, never the file. A file someone else changed is not written over, and the next turn says so; a turn whose folder has gone is refused. How long a turn takes is below.
- **One app writes a World at a time.** While a World is open, a hidden lock file beside it (`.<name>.world.lock`) keeps any other window or World Machine (the demo and the full app share a Worlds folder) from opening it to play or renaming or removing it from Home; Home renames and exports an open World through its window. The operating system lets go of the lock if the app crashes. A sync tool or a copy put back by hand does not take the lock: the open World then refuses its next turn as "changed on disk", as before.
- **Two speed and size bars are measured, not met, and the turn's 25 ms mark is not reached on the measuring machine.** On a three-year harbour in a release build of v0.29, on a shared 4-core Linux machine at load 1–2:
  - the first look after a day (the snapshot alone) has medians of 36.7, 21.5 and 19.3 ms in three rounds (bar 20 ms);
  - the app holds 108.6 MB after 20 days of play, from 92.6 MB just opened (bar 100 MB);
  - a turn with its save: on the speed test's own file the median is 26–27 ms (bar 30 / 35 ms). The repo's turn bench takes 35–37 ms, against the 24.5–24.8 ms the v0.28 review measured on another file on a faster day. On the same file, interleaved, v0.28 takes 35.4–38.9 ms and v0.29 36.6–37.7 ms, and callgrind counts 11% fewer instructions for v0.29 over the same twelve turns, so this is no regression; but the bench does not reach 25 ms on this machine.

  The nightly reports all three without failing. Most of a turn is the first look after it (inspectors, moments, commands and the town worked out afresh); the regression of v0.27 was new work in that look (lines about things people enjoyed, recounted from the whole history every turn; the town's layout; plots), and v0.28 counts the enjoyed lines on from where it stood, frees the old look away from the turn, and stops copying the season's events and the cover's drawings for every save. The memory growth is the allocator's high-water mark after the first save, with the World's history held both as events and as the file's text.
- **A builder's three-year World code is 49,179 characters,** under the 50,000 bar by less than 2%. A denser code format would give it room.
- **At night the drawer, cards and pages are paper under a lamp** (in the light appearance), while what hangs over the scene (the gauges, buttons, names and speech) is dark as the night sky. Seen on Linux, not on a Mac.
- **Moment strips draw only the design of the place a moment is at** (a shop's sign, a home's quilt, a flag or a sail), not designs elsewhere in town.
- **Designing, naming, plots, the touchable strip and a friend's resident are untried on a Mac.** They were checked in the Linux preview, keys and clicks included.
- **Homes and works cannot be inspected.** They are drawn and named, but clicking one opens nothing, because they are worked out for the picture rather than recorded in the World.
- **A visitor on a World code does not see when works were built,** since a code carries only its latest season.
- **A favour is one at a time and asked only while you are there.** People ask you to look in on someone, invite someone out, cheer someone up or pass on an apology, and you do it by talking to that person or with the one-click reply in their card (a click sends the favour itself, never words a model reads). A favour not done in three days lapses quietly; asks slow down after lapses but never stop, so a player who never talks is still asked about every three weeks. The thanks show on the asker's card and over their head. Done in your own words, on fresh29's favour phrasing, 88.9% of favours are understood as done (v0.29's bar 80%, a step toward 90%); an apology passed on without a name counts when an apology favour is open for that person.
- **The demo is 20 to 30 minutes of Tiny Society and ends on day 8,** with a farewell at dusk and a wishlist link (`WORLD_MACHINE_WISHLIST_URL` at build time; the project's page until there is a store page). On the morning of day 4, letting the day pass lets the town live three days on its own instead (its real background days, as when the app is closed; the demo skips the six-hour wait), and day 7 opens on the return film. The pacing is `apps/world-machine-desktop/demo/pacing.json`, walked by `tests/demo_walk.rs`. It is the full app otherwise, for Tiny Society only; the World it keeps opens in the full app and carries on. It has been built and run in the Linux preview, where the real window reaches the ending card, not on a Mac or Windows. At the intended pace it is 24.8 minutes; a player who answers first is asked a favour at 94 s, but at the intended pace the first favour comes at about 12 minutes (748 s), against a goal of about 3. No think-aloud tester has played it, and the art director has not signed off the demo build on its own.

## The look: accepted v0.29 deviations

The art director signed off v0.29's free play on 2026-10-09, after four rounds, with these deviations ([ART_DIRECTION](ART_DIRECTION.md#v029-sign-off)). They were seen in frames from a release build on Linux.

**The harbour**
1. The dusk sky is flat beige-pink and the water grey-green, with no long warm shadows; the gold glaze on the land and the lit lamps carry it.
2. A milky veil lies over the land at noon and in rain.
3. On long pans, far buildings show the rough painting mid-glide (blurred at 1.0 s), never on landing.
4. Cards clip people beside the speaker: Leo's Find card clips the third man of his group, and on day 13 Leo's card covers the white-bearded man at the stalls.
5. People overlap within groups and stand in front of the lighthouse door; five heads on day 21 read as 3 + 2.
6. A card ghosts for a frame while it re-docks from right to left after the return film.
7. A home is cut at the left edge on day 1.
8. Three return-film beats have weak subjects and no bubble: "harbour lamp finished" (a field of lamps), "fish to the mainland", and "note under the door" (no door in view).
9. A lamp post stands inside the quay scaffold.
10. Mara's opening line on day 21 is sometimes missing at 8 s; it depends on timing.

**Pocket Universe**

11. The places share a template: lamps in a row, two roped plots and a lone keeper. The rule of four residents on the first screen is waived.
12. Maple Street has a lime haze at noon.
13. Ares's land at night stays rust-lit rather than going dark; the left-middle plain is empty, and the leftmost lamp sits at the edge.
14. The first day's free "Make something" offer opens by itself after the greeting, at about 5–8 s and not every time. It covers the upper-right sky (the moon at night), and the keeper's line about building a home does not match the ice bench it offers.

**Icebridge**

15. Piko's bubble at noon covers the bridge's deck and the top of its arch.
16. The nests sit about 30 px from the left edge at noon and night: whole, but tight.
17. Water shows only partly through the arch; at night the arch's inside is a pale lit panel.
18. Faint marks on the ice field, visible only with the levels raised: two vertical hairlines, a light smear below the nests, and the plaza's front rope right on the lead's edge.
19. At night the keeper's bubble after the offer partly covers the lower nest's fish rack.

## Verification gaps

- **The nightly has not yet been green three nights running.** It was red every night from 1 to 7 October, on v0.26 code, and its first run on v0.27 code (8 October, `b90e3eb`) was green in both jobs. It runs only on `main`, so none has run on v0.29 code. The window's longest-frame bar (8 ms) passes on GitHub's runners but failed 4 of 10 local runs on a 4-core machine at v0.29 (and 9 of 15 on a quiet one at v0.28): the slow frames are the app's own work.
- **Zooming out shows the rough painting for seconds.** Since v0.29 "painted" is counted per region, and after Find, return beats and a place opening the rough is on the subject for at most 241 ms (bar 250 ms). Zooming out into the whole town is the exception: 1.5–2.2 s on the subject and up to 2.4 s anywhere (bar 600 ms), so the release check's `zoom` scenario failed in 3 of 3 runs at v0.29's measurement. Mid-glide on a long pan, far buildings are rough for a moment; never on landing.
- **The release screenshot run is new to releases.** Since v0.29 the release workflow runs `release-shots.sh` itself and publishes nothing unless it passes; v0.29.0 is its first release.
- **Windows has not been played on a real PC.** CI runs clippy and the tests on Windows, the language comes from the system and keys are kept in Credential Manager, but nobody has played the first 30 minutes on a Windows machine. The `steam` feature is a spike, off by default.
- **Blind set 7 for the World voice is unmeasured.** Its prompts are written; measuring the judge needs a live key, and none was available. The voice's figures above are v0.28's.
- **Nobody has tried the demo aloud.** The plan's five think-aloud testers on the v0.29 demo have not played it.
- **The store shots are v0.28's.** They have not been captured again, so `shot-2-return.png` and the dusk shot still do not match what a player sees at that moment.
- **Small things seen in the Linux preview:** the Home window shows through for 0.1–0.3 s after a World window appears, and the drawer's tooltip covers its first row.
- The pre-alpha has been exercised through automated tests and CI-built screenshots, not yet on a wide range of real Macs. Layout on small screens and with large accessibility text sizes is unverified.
- **Nothing that needs a keypress or a click is verified anywhere.** The CI screenshot runner can launch the app and open a World, but GitHub's macOS runners grant no Accessibility permission, so no keystroke it sends ever arrives — proven by pressing ⌘M, which the app binds, and watching the window not minimize. Full screen, every keyboard shortcut, and every button click are therefore unchecked until somebody runs the app on a real Mac.
- **Linux preview is not a Mac.** Since `v0.6.0`, `scripts/linux-preview.sh` runs the real app under Xvfb and every screenshot in [PRODUCT_REVIEW.md](PRODUCT_REVIEW.md) comes from it, clicks and keys included. Fonts, weights and window chrome differ from macOS, and the Pack install review has not been seen at all, because the preview cannot drive a file picker.
- The CI screenshot runner produced images with layout but no text through `v0.5.0`. That was not a limitation of the runner: the app itself rendered no text anywhere, on CI and on real Macs alike, and `v0.5.1` fixes it. Screenshots in the repository are still taken by hand on a real Mac.
